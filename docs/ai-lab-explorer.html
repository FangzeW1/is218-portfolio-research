AI Lab Explorer

A career-intelligence application for discovering AI labs, engineering teams, real opportunities, and verified public recruiting channels—without depending entirely on job boards.

← Current roadmap · Planning stage · November 19, 2026

The problem and goal

AI engineering opportunities can be scattered across employer sites, lab pages, and job boards. Titles vary, some listings are stale, and it can be hard to discover teams worth approaching or determine whether a role is realistic for an early-career candidate.

Goal: turn public employer information into an evidence-backed, searchable map of teams, opportunities, and relevant career paths.

Primary user: a student or early-career engineer looking for AI application, platform, or forward-deployed engineering opportunities. Other users are possible but unvalidated.

What someone could do

  1. Search companies and discover their AI labs or engineering teams.
  2. View official team pages, publicly listed openings, and verified recruiting channels.
  3. Filter opportunities by role type, location, experience requirements, and available evidence.
  4. Compare a job's requirements with stated skills and portfolio projects.
  5. Explore how companies, labs, teams, and roles relate through an optional interactive graph.

Example: Search for entry-level AI application roles near New Jersey or remote. Explorer surfaces employer pages, clearly labels unverified eligibility, and explains why a role may match a candidate's Python, web, API, or RAG experience.

Four useful capabilities

Organization discovery

Connect company → AI lab → engineering team → official website and published research.

Opportunity tracking

Show job title, location, source, verification date, and whether the listing still appears active.

Evidence-based matching

Explain fit using explicit requirements and portfolio evidence; flag missing skills rather than inventing qualifications.

Public contact channels

Link to official recruiting pages, public professional contacts, or published team channels—never guessed private email addresses.

Proposed architecture

Discovery: start with a curated set of organizations and their official careers, lab, and team pages.

Extraction: normalize companies, teams, roles, locations, requirements, and source URLs; attach a last-checked timestamp.

Storage and search: keep structured records in a database, support filtering, and optionally use embeddings for semantic matching.

AI layer: summarize role fit and answer questions using retrieved source text; always link back to the underlying listing.

Interface: mobile-friendly search or conversational discovery, with concise company/role cards and optional organization graph.

Possible technologies (not decided)

A web frontend, backend API, relational database, structured extraction, optional vector search, and a graph visualization library. Begin with reliable manual or permitted official-source ingestion before considering broad crawling.

Build it as a group

  1. First playable: a small, curated directory of verified AI teams and job listings with source links and filters.
  2. Useful matching: add clear requirement comparisons and explainable fit indicators.
  3. Organization relationships: connect companies, labs, teams, and jobs in an optional graph.
  4. Later: refresh checks, saved searches, change detection, and richer AI-assisted research.

Group workflow: agree on a persona and three to five user stories, define source/verification rules, divide responsibilities, and cross-review results before expanding scope.

Group not yet formed. No code, repository, or assigned roles are implied.

Data quality, ethics, and safety

Verification: store the original URL and check date. Mark unavailable, stale, or uncertain listings; never promise a job is open without checking.

Contact privacy: use only genuinely public professional channels; do not guess personal emails, scrape restricted personal data, or automate unsolicited outreach.

Access rules: respect site terms, robots guidance, rate limits, and authorized API access.

Matching limits: a missing experience requirement does not automatically mean entry level; explain uncertainty and avoid overstating eligibility.

Accessibility: make role cards, filters, and any graph usable on mobile and with keyboard/screen readers.

How to prove usefulness

Evaluate whether records match their official sources, how often links become stale, whether filters work, and whether matching explanations cite actual job requirements. Test with realistic early-career searches.

Measure value: time needed to find several relevant official listings versus searching manually; verified-source coverage; false or outdated matches.

Show employers: data model, extraction/refresh pipeline, API, frontend, tests, source provenance, architecture, and individual contributions.

Relevant skills: full-stack engineering, data ingestion, search, AI integration, information quality, privacy, and collaborative product development.

Status and decisions to make with the group

Status: highest-priority portfolio concept and intended group project; awaiting a proper group before implementation.

Open questions: initial company set, target role categories, refresh method, permitted data sources, how matching will be evaluated, and whether the graph belongs in the first release.

Guiding principle: fewer trustworthy, useful results beat a large directory of unverified claims.
